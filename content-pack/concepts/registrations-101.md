## What it is

A newcomer would assume that an application is a document, finished and then sent. Before anything can be sent the organisation has to exist in several federal systems, and the first is SAM.gov, the registry run by the General Services Administration, where it must hold an active registration to apply as a prime applicant [1897] [1899]. Registration assigns the Unique Entity ID (UEI), which every later registration uses, and a domestic organisation also receives a CAGE code [1898] [784]. SAM.gov states that there is no charge to get a UEI, to register or to maintain a registration [1898].

Most agencies then receive applications through Grants.gov, and some add a system of their own, such as eRA Commons at NIH or Research.gov at NSF [1895] [1911] [161]. The Department of Justice takes a first form through Grants.gov and the full application in its own JustGrants [619]. NIH describes the SAM.gov entity administrator as the chief financial officer or an authorising official [1909]. The person named in SAM.gov as Electronic Business Point of Contact registers the organisation on Grants.gov and assigns roles, and someone must hold the Authorized Organization Representative role to submit [1895]. At NIH a Signing Official registers the organisation in eRA Commons [1911] [1909]. SAM.gov and Grants.gov are entered with a Login.gov account, which belongs to a person and not to the organisation [1897] [1965] [1932].

The registry gives its own activation time as up to 10 business days, and Grants.gov warns of weeks or months where information is not available or verified [1897] [1895]. NIH's planning figures are three weeks or more for a first SAM registration, two weeks or more for eRA Commons, one business day or more for Grants.gov, and six weeks or more for the whole set [1909]. NSF advises starting SAM registration at least 90 days before submission [161]. The SAM.gov registration lapses unless renewed every 365 days, and under 2 CFR 25.205 an agency may not issue an award, or add funds to one, to an entity out of compliance [1897] [1899]. A Grants.gov account is deactivated after 365 days of inactivity; NIH states no renewal period for eRA Commons, though the SAM.gov registration it depends on is renewed yearly [1966] [1909].

Every PD/PI, senior/key person and other significant contributor named on an NIH application needs a personal eRA Commons account, created through the organisation's Signing Official or account administrator, and at NSF each person holds one NSF ID [1910] [156]. For due dates on or after January 25, 2026 NIH requires an ORCID iD, a persistent identifier that is free to the researcher, of every person who submits a Common Form, linked to the eRA Commons account [31] [1950]. The Common Forms are two government-wide disclosure forms, the Biographical Sketch and the Current and Pending (Other) Support form; they are produced in SciENcv, and NIH states that there is no blank template outside it [249] [1943] [1942]. An organisation that will only be a subrecipient needs less: under 2 CFR 25.300 it must have a UEI and is not required to complete full registration, though with a UEI alone it cannot apply directly for federal awards [1899] [1900] [1897]. A person applying on their own behalf through Grants.gov adds an Individual Applicant profile and does not register in SAM.gov [1896].

## The key idea in one picture

Picture three steps rising to a door marked *submit*. The lowest step is labelled *SAM.gov: UEI and entity registration*, with the entity administrator standing on it [1897] [1909]. The middle step is *Grants.gov: organisation profile and roles*, with the Electronic Business Point of Contact on it and the Authorized Organization Representative beside the door [1895]. The top step is *the agency's own system*, drawn for NIH as *eRA Commons* with the Signing Official [1911]. Under each step hang two tags, a clock for the stated wait and a loop for renewal. On the first they read *up to 10 business days* and *every 365 days* [1897]. On the second they read *one business day or more*, NIH's estimate, and *account deactivated after 365 days of inactivity* [1909] [1966]. On the third they read *two weeks or more* and *no renewal period stated* [1909].

Beside the steps stands a short ladder for the person, its rungs labelled *Login.gov account*, *agency account*, *ORCID iD* and *biosketch in SciENcv* [1910] [31] [1942]. Low on the first step is a side door marked *UEI only*, where a subrecipient enters; it does not lead to *submit* [1899] [1897].

## How this guide uses it

The section on the gates and the route for getting registered before a first federal application rest on this idea, and every other federal route lists the gate records it depends on. Each gate record gives who does the step, the time stated, the cost and the renewal, and the registration-times figure sets the agencies' planning figures side by side.

The Simpler.Grants.gov roadmap gives 2027 as the stage at which that site replaces legacy Grants.gov and becomes the default experience, with no day or month [1919]. GSA asked for comment, until March 30, 2026, on updating the financial assistance representations and certifications made in SAM.gov, in a notice that states no effective date [1866]. OMB's proposed rule of May 29, 2026 names 2 CFR part 25, the part that requires a UEI and SAM.gov registration, among the parts it would revise [1458] [1899]. It remains a proposal, and section 157 of Public Law 119-103 provides that a rule arising from it shall not be issued, finalised or take effect through December 11, 2026 [1858] [1480].
