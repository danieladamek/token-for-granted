## What it is

A newcomer tends to call any money from a federal agency a grant, and to assume that getting one means beating other applicants. Federal law treats an award as a legal instrument and gives an agency three of them, the choice following from the purpose of the transaction [1851]. A procurement contract acquires property or services for the Government's own direct benefit or use [1851]. A grant and a cooperative agreement both transfer something of value to carry out a public purpose that a law authorises [1851].

Those two differ on one point: whether substantial involvement is expected between the agency and the recipient while the work is carried out [1851]. The statute does not define that involvement, so a notice for a cooperative agreement has to describe what the agency expects [1848]. At NIH the U01 funds a research project under a cooperative agreement [49] [3]. One U01 notice says that after award NIH staff "will assist, guide, coordinate, or participate in project activities", and it sends disputes to a three-member panel [116].

A second division cuts across the first. The Uniform Guidance defines a discretionary award as one in which the agency, under statutory authority to exercise judgment, selects the recipient or the amount through a competitive process or on the merit of proposals [1894]. A non-discretionary award goes to specific recipients under statutory requirements, such that the agency cannot exercise judgment [1894]. Grants.gov's glossary describes a mandatory grant, where the authorising statute requires an award to each eligible entity, and a formula grant, an allocation to states, territories or local governments by a formula in the law [1880]. A discretionary award is competed before a federal agency; formula money is reached, if at all, through a state or local recipient [1880] [1805]. Each programme's entry in the Assistance Listings, the government-wide source of information on federal financial assistance programmes, states which kind its awards are [1808]. A project named by Congress is a further case. Congress may name a recipient, a project and an amount in an appropriations act, which the House calls Community Project Funding and the Senate Congressionally Directed Spending [1667] [1674]. No organisation applies to either committee, and a named recipient still applies to the agency before money moves [1667] [1674] [1686].

Whatever the instrument, what arrives is a document. At NIH the Notice of Award is the official award document, and the recipient accepts it by drawing down or requesting funds [122]. The period of performance is the time between the start and end date of an award and may contain one or more budget periods; a budget period is the funded portion during which the recipient is authorised to incur financial obligations [1799]. Both pairs of dates are printed in the award document [1810]. An R01 is generally awarded for one to five budget periods, each normally 12 months [42]. Funding for each later budget period is a continuation: one HHS notice has recipients file a non-competing continuation application for each later budget period, and NIH's annual progress report is due about 45 or about 60 days before the next one starts, depending on the award [1218] [1954]. A renewal is different. An R01 "can be renewed by competing for an additional project period", and a renewal award begins a new and distinct period of performance [42] [1816].

## The key idea in one picture

Picture two questions, one drawn above the other. The upper one asks what the transaction is for, and it has two branches. Where the Government acquires property or services for its own direct benefit or use, the branch ends in a box labelled procurement contract [1851]. Where something of value is transferred for a public purpose, the branch forks once more on whether substantial involvement is expected, into a box labelled grant where it is not and a box labelled cooperative agreement where it is [1851].

The lower question asks who chooses the recipient, and three boxes stand side by side beneath it. In the box labelled discretionary the agency selects from among eligible applicants [1880]. In the box labelled mandatory or formula the authorising statute sets who is paid and how much, and an arrow runs on to a state, territory or local government [1880]. In the third box Congress names the recipient: an arrow enters from the office of a House Member or a Senator, and another leaves towards the agency, where the named recipient still applies [1667] [1674] [1686]. An award has a place on each line at once [1851] [1880].

## How this guide uses it

Each programme record names the instrument it funds by, and formula programmes carry their own status label. The section on how grant money moves starts from the two divisions drawn here, and the section on programme and service money follows the formula box to the state. The routes divide along the lower line as well, with one route kept for a project named by Congress. The figure of the four doors adds foundations to the three boxes of that line. The primer on reading a funding notice takes up the discretionary box from this point.

Two things were unsettled on October 5, 2026. OMB's proposed rule of May 29, 2026 would revise 2 CFR 200.201, the section on the choice of instrument, and would remove one form of grant, the fixed amount award [1803]. It is a proposal: the continuing resolution approved September 2, 2026 bars a rule arising from it from being issued or finalised through December 11, 2026 [1480]. Under the same resolution NIH states that its institutes may issue non-competing awards "at a level below that indicated on the most recent Notice of Award" [83].
