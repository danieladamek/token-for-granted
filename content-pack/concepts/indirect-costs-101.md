## What it is

A newcomer drawing up a first budget tends to treat the award as the cost of the project: the salaries and the supplies. Those are direct costs, the ones that can be identified specifically with the award [1835]. On top of them come indirect costs: costs incurred for a common or joint purpose that cannot readily be assigned to the award that benefits [1799]. At major universities and major non-profits they fall into two groups, Facilities and Administration, which gives the other name, F&A costs [1802].

The second amount is worked out as a percentage, the indirect cost rate, applied to a base of direct costs [1849] [1850]. An organisation gets its rate in one of two ways [1802]. One is a negotiated indirect cost rate agreement, settled by a single federal agency, the organisation's cognizant agency for indirect costs, on behalf of all the others, which must then accept it [1799] [1802]. For a university that agency is HHS or the Office of Naval Research; for a non-profit it is the agency with the largest dollar value of awards made directly to the organisation [1849] [1850]. The other way is the de minimis rate: an organisation with no current negotiated rate may elect up to 15 percent of modified total direct costs, without documentation, and use it indefinitely [1802]. Agency pages do not all carry that figure, and the NIH policy text as fetched gives 10% [64].

The base is normally modified total direct costs, or MTDC [1799]. It takes in salaries and wages, fringe benefits, materials and supplies, services, travel, and up to the first $50,000 of each subaward [1799]. Equipment, capital expenditures, patient care charges, rental costs, tuition remission, scholarships and fellowships, participant support costs and the rest of each subaward are left out [1799].

A negotiated rate does not reach every award. An agency may use a different rate when a federal statute or regulation requires it, and HHS regulations limit indirect costs on training grants to 8% of MTDC [1802] [1884]. NIH pays that 8% on K awards and T32 training grants [744] [750]. Its individual fellowships and S10 instrument awards pay none [736] [1536]. Foundations follow no common rule, and each sets its own figure in its own policy [2129] [2156]. Gates allows U.S. universities up to 10% of budgeted total direct costs, and Ford sets a minimum of 25 percent on its project grants [2257] [2332].

## The maths, gently

$$ \text{indirect costs} = \text{rate} \times \text{base}, \qquad \text{base} = \text{MTDC} $$

- **Indirect costs**: the amount the award pays on top of direct costs [1849] [1850].
- **Rate**: the organisation's negotiated rate, or the de minimis rate of up to 15 percent [1802].
- **Base**: modified total direct costs, the direct costs that remain once the excluded items are taken out [1799].

A worked example, with illustrative direct costs. An organisation with no negotiated rate budgets $100,000 for salaries, fringe benefits, supplies and travel, $20,000 for one piece of equipment and $80,000 for one subaward; all three figures are illustrative. The equipment is left out of the base, and only the first $50,000 of the subaward counts [1799]. The base is therefore $150,000. At a de minimis rate of 15 percent, the most the rule allows, the indirect costs are $22,500 [1802]. Direct costs remain $200,000 in all, and the award comes to $222,500.

## How this guide uses it

Section 3 of the main text sets out these rules. The programme records state the indirect cost terms of each programme, the routes for training awards and foundation grants depend on the limits described here, and two figures, indirect-cost-rules and foundation-indirect-rates, set the federal rules, the 2025 caps and the foundations' figures side by side.

The second 15 percent is contested. In 2025 four agencies announced that they would pay universities indirect costs at 15 percent in place of negotiated rates, and the District of Massachusetts set each policy aside [24] [1852] [237] [1855]. NIH's notice of February 7, 2025 was enjoined and later vacated, and the First Circuit affirmed on January 5, 2026 [24]. DOE's policy of April 11, 2025 was vacated on June 30, 2025, and DOE declared it no longer in effect from January 23, 2026 [1852] [289]. NSF's notice of May 2, 2025 was vacated on June 20, 2025; a case summary records that the First Circuit dismissed NSF's appeal, on NSF's own motion, on September 30, 2025 [1854] [239]. The Department of Defense memoranda of May 14 and June 12, 2025 were vacated on October 10, 2025, with a permanent injunction for the university plaintiffs and the member universities of the plaintiff associations; a plaintiff association reports that the Government's appeal was dismissed on February 18, 2026 [1855] [292]. Executive Order 14332 directs OMB to revise the Uniform Guidance to limit facilities and administrative costs, and no such revision has taken effect [246] [1480].
