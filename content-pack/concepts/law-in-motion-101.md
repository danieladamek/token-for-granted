## What it is

A newcomer would take an agency's announcement for the rule. In 2025 and 2026 an announcement was often one layer of law among several, and another layer undid it. Congress enacts statutes, numbered as public laws; Public Law 119-103 was approved on September 2, 2026 [1480]. A regulation sits in the Code of Federal Regulations, where the Uniform Guidance is 2 CFR part 200, and a federal statute governs where it conflicts with that part [1805]. A regulation changes through the Federal Register: the text of part 200 in force was published there on April 22, 2024 at 89 FR 30046 and took effect on October 1, 2024 [1883].

A proposed rule is a draft published for comment. OMB published one on May 29, 2026, at 91 FR 32198, to revise 2 CFR; comments were due July 13, 2026, and the Federal Register record shows 496,606 of them [1458] [1858]. Until a final rule is published, the proposal applies to no one [1480] [1803]. An executive order is the President's instruction to agencies: Executive Order 14332, signed August 7, 2025, directs OMB to revise the Uniform Guidance, and no such revision has taken effect [246] [1480]. An agency notice states that agency's policy. NIH's notice of February 7, 2025 announced a standard 15% indirect cost rate in place of negotiated rates, and DOE, NSF and the Department of Defense announced the same rate for institutions of higher education on April 11, May 2 and May 14, 2025 [25] [1852] [237] [1855].

An injunction is a court order that bars an agency from acting, for the parties the order covers; a vacatur sets the policy itself aside. The District of Massachusetts enjoined NIH's notice on March 5, 2025, later made the injunction permanent and vacated the notice, and on appeal the First Circuit affirmed on January 5, 2026 [24]. The same court vacated NSF's policy on June 20, 2025 without an injunction [1854]. It enjoined DOE's on May 15, 2025, in an order not limited to the named plaintiffs, and vacated it on June 30, 2025 [1853] [1852]. It vacated the Defense policy on October 10, 2025 and made its injunction permanent for the university plaintiffs and the member universities of the plaintiff associations [1855]. The HHS notices of March 24 and 25, 2025 terminated about $11 billion in CDC and SAMHSA public health grants [544]. The injunction of May 16, 2025, granted to twenty-three states and the District of Columbia, bars HHS "from enforcing or implementing the public health terminations for Plaintiff States and their local health jurisdictions" [544].

An appropriations act is the statute that funds agencies for a fiscal year, and it can carry conditions: section 224 of the FY 2026 act, Public Law 119-75, keeps the indirect cost provisions of 45 CFR part 75 applying to NIH grants [62]. A continuing resolution extends the prior year's acts for a limited time, as Public Law 119-103 does through December 11, 2026 [1480]. A budget request is a proposal to Congress. The FY 2027 President's Budget proposes a 15 percent cap on F&A costs for each NIH award [57]. In May 2025 the White House proposed eliminating the Legal Services Corporation in FY 2026, and Congress passed $540 million for it [1329].

## The key idea in one picture

Picture a time line from January 2025 to December 2026, with a row for each contested rule and a mark for each event, shaped by who acted: an agency, a court, Congress or the President. The row for NIH's indirect cost cap reads: notice, February 7, 2025; injunction, March 5, 2025; affirmed on appeal, January 5, 2026; appropriations act with section 224, signed February 3, 2026 [25] [24] [62]. The row for the Uniform Guidance reads: proposed rule, May 29, 2026; comments closed, July 13, 2026; section 157 of Public Law 119-103, September 2, 2026. A shaded band then runs to December 11, 2026, the date through which no rule arising from the proposal may be issued or finalised [1458] [1480].

Beside each mark stands a status word from the guide's change records, and each word is learned most easily from a record that carries it. *Announced* marks a statement of plans, such as the three tranches of FY 2027 competitions IES set out on August 6, 2026 [661]. *Proposed* marks OMB's rule and the FY 2027 budget requests [1458] [57]. *Enacted* marks an appropriation Congress has passed, *final* an action issued and standing, such as the NIH award terms effective October 1, 2025, and *pending-implementation* an action issued with a later start, such as NIH's letters of collaboration, due in fiscal year 2027 [1329] [65] [131]. *Enjoined* marks the HHS terminations and *vacated* the NIH cap [544] [24]. *In-litigation* marks a dispute still before a court, such as the suit Tennessee and Students for Fair Admissions filed on June 11, 2025 over three Education Department programmes [1607]. *On-appeal* marks a ruling under review: the Ninth Circuit ruled in Thakur v. Trump on May 26, 2026, and the government petitioned the Supreme Court in August 2026 [242] [243]. *Rescinded* marks a step its issuer withdrew, as NIH did on June 12, 2025 with a civil rights award term added on April 21, 2025 [1870] [1871]. *Frozen-by-appropriations* marks section 157 [1480].

## How this guide uses it

Section 10 of the main text, the watch notes on routes, the pending-change lines on the guide's records and every entry in its ledger of changes use these words, and the figure changes-timeline draws them. Each status is printed with its date, since a contested rule can change standing within weeks.

Several of these cases were still moving when the guide closed. Section 157 runs only through December 11, 2026, and no enacted fiscal year 2027 appropriations act was found [1480] [1873]. NSF awards issued on or after May 5, 2025 carry a term applying its 15 percent policy if NSF is later permitted to apply it [154]. A litigation tracker reports that the HHS termination case has been stayed since October 27, 2025 with the injunction in effect [1210].
