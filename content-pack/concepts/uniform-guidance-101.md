## What it is

A newcomer tends to read a federal award as an arrangement with one agency, and to expect the rules for spending the money to be that agency's own. Most of them are shared. The Uniform Guidance is the government-wide set of administrative requirements, cost principles and audit requirements for federal awards, issued by the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) as 2 CFR part 200 [1799] [1805]. It binds the agencies that make awards and, through the terms of each award, recipients and subrecipients [1805].

The part follows an award from start to finish. Before any money moves it fixes what a notice of funding opportunity must contain and in what order [1800] [1848]. It has the agency review the risk an applicant poses, a review that can end in an award with extra conditions attached [1809] [1886]. It then lists what the award document must carry, the termination provisions among them [1810].

Once the award is made, the cost principles decide what it may pay for: a cost has to be allowable, and to be allowable it has to be reasonable and allocable, among other tests [1831]. Some changes, such as a change in scope or in key personnel, need prior approval, which is the agency's written approval before the recipient acts [1815]. A recipient that hands part of the work to another organisation by subaward takes on a list of duties toward it, and the award terms flow down [1823] [1805]. Financial reports are collected no less than annually and no more often than quarterly [1820]. A non-federal entity that expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards during its fiscal year must have a single audit, which is filed with the Federal Audit Clearinghouse [1845] [1846].

The part also governs the end of an award. Section 200.340 lists four ways an award may be terminated: the recipient's failure to comply, the recipient's consent, the recipient's own written notice, and the terms of the award itself [1801]. At closeout a recipient submits all reports and liquidates its financial obligations no later than 120 calendar days after the end of the period of performance [1830]. Where a federal statute or regulation conflicts with the part, the statute or regulation governs [1805].

## The key idea in one picture

The picture is a stack of three slabs, widest at the bottom. The bottom slab is labelled *2 CFR part 200, the Uniform Guidance (OMB)* and is divided into six compartments, A to F: definitions, general provisions, pre-award, post-award, cost principles, audit [1805]. The middle slab is labelled *the agency's adoption and its policy guide*; each agency adopts the part in its own chapter of 2 CFR, and HHS, for example, adopts it with additions [1884]. Two boxes sit inside this slab. One reads *NIH Grants Policy Statement*, whose requirements a recipient agrees to by accepting an NIH award unless the notice of award states otherwise [118] [35]. The other reads *NSF PAPPG*, the Proposal and Award Policies and Procedures Guide, which has twelve chapters in two parts, proposals and awards [148]. The top slab, the smallest, is labelled *the award document and its terms* [1810].

An arrow rises from the bottom slab through the middle one, labelled *one rule, restated*. Its example is the extension of an award: 200.308 lets an agency allow a recipient to start a one-time extension of up to 12 months on notice at least 10 calendar days before the period ends, and the NSF guide gives a recipient that extension on the same notice [1815] [259]. A second arrow leaves the top slab for a box marked *subaward* and is labelled *terms flow down* [1805]. Beside the bottom slab a box with a dashed outline reads *proposed replacement, May 29, 2026: not final* [1858] [1480].

## How this guide uses it

Section 3 of the main text, *The rules underneath*, rests on this idea. So do the mechanics records that cite a section of part 200, the routes for federal awards and for joining an award as a subrecipient, and the changes-timeline figure, which places the 2024 revision and the 2026 proposal by date.

The text in force when the pack was checked on October 4, 2026 is the revision published April 22, 2024 and effective October 1, 2024 [1883] [1801]. On May 29, 2026 a proposed rule from OMB, with 44 agencies listed on it, was published; it would issue the requirements as a regulation and apply OMB's amendments government-wide on the effective date of a final rule [1858] [1803]. No final rule had been published by October 4, 2026, and OMB's regulatory agenda of August 14, 2026 lists the rule in the proposed rule stage [1858] [1859]. Section 157 of the continuing resolution approved September 2, 2026 provides that through December 11, 2026 a rule arising out of the proposal, "or a substantially similar rule, shall not be issued or finalized" [1480]. Until a final rule exists the proposal applies to no one [1480] [1803]. One of the agency guides is itself under revision: NSF published for comment a draft Guidance on Financial Assistance to replace the PAPPG, and comments closed August 24, 2026 [152] [147].
