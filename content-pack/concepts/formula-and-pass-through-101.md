## What it is

A non-profit that finds a federal programme paying for its kind of work tends to assume that the next step is an application to the agency named on the page. On the formula route no such step exists [1292] [1259]. Federal awards divide by how the recipient and the amount are chosen [1880]. For a discretionary grant the agency may select the recipient from among all eligible applicants and decides the amount [1880]. A formula grant is an allocation to states, territories or local governments by a distribution formula in the law or regulations [1880]. Agencies call some of these block grants, a term for which the Grants.gov glossary had no entry when read on October 4, 2026 [1292] [1880].

The body that receives the money then funds local organisations, and the Uniform Guidance names each party [1292] [1799]. A pass-through entity is a recipient or subrecipient that gives a subaward to another organisation to carry out part of a federal programme [1799]. The organisation that receives the subaward is a subrecipient, and the award terms flow down to it [1799] [1805]. A non-profit funded through a state block or formula grant is a subrecipient of the state [1823] [1880].

HUD's page for the Community Development Block Grant says so in plain words: "HUD does not provide CDBG assistance directly to individuals, businesses, nonprofit or organizations or other non-governmental entities." [1292] FEMA's FY 2026 notice for the Nonprofit Security Grant Program reads: "Nonprofit organizations may NOT apply directly to the Department of Homeland Security (DHS)/FEMA." [1320] The Administration for Community Living writes that formula grants "require no application or competition" [1259]. The two SAMHSA block grants, HRSA's Title V block grant and the Education Department's 21st Century Community Learning Centers are awarded to states in the same way [1130] [1168] [1399]. Past that point the federal pages read for the health block grants do not describe how a provider applies to its state [1129] [1168] [1219].

Under 2 CFR 200.332 a pass-through entity verifies in SAM.gov that the subrecipient is not excluded, identifies the subaward as a subaward with 14 data items, evaluates the subrecipient's risk of fraud and of noncompliance, monitors its activities, reviews its reports and verifies that it is audited when the rules require [1823] [2105]. A state that distributes a formula grant has the same list of duties as a university that leads a multi-site project [1823]. The guidance does not reach every such grant in full: 2 CFR 200.101(e) takes block grants authorised by the Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1981 out of subparts C, D and E of the guidance, except for specified provisions [1805].

## The key idea in one picture

Three boxes stand in a row, joined by two arrows. The left box is labelled *federal agency*. The middle box is labelled *state, territory, tribe or local government* and, beneath that, *pass-through entity*; the right box is labelled *local organisation* and *subrecipient* [1799] [1823]. The first arrow carries the words *formula or block grant, no competition* and the second *subaward, on the state body's own calendar* [1259] [1399] [1320]. A dashed line from the right box straight back to the left box is crossed out and marked *no application* [1292] [1320]. A band beneath both arrows reads *federal award terms flow down* [1805]. The chain can be longer: Older Americans Act money passes from ACL to 56 state agencies on aging, then to 618 area agencies on aging, then to nearly 20,000 local service providers [1260].

Three tags hang from the right box. The first, *Unique Entity ID*, marks that a subrecipient must have the identifier before a subaward can be made and is not required to complete full SAM.gov registration; the identifier is free [1899] [1900] [1898]. The second, *indirect cost rate*, marks that the pass-through entity must accept a subrecipient's federally negotiated rate, and that without one the rate is negotiated between the two parties or is the de minimis rate of up to 15 percent of modified total direct costs [1823] [1802]. The third, *audit*, marks that money received as a subrecipient counts as federal awards expended, and that an entity expending $1,000,000 or more in federal awards in its fiscal year must have a single audit, while one below that amount is exempt from federal audit requirements for that year [1845].

## How this guide uses it

The section on funding a programme or a service, the route for formula money through the state, the route for joining another body's application, and each programme record marked as pass-through rest on this idea. Such a record names the body a non-profit goes to and cites the federal page that says so. The guide stops at that page and does not describe any state's own process.
<!-- framing -->

OMB's proposed rule of May 29, 2026 would revise 200.331 and 200.332, eliminate fixed amount subawards and require pass-through entities to report subawards on SAM.gov [1803]. It remains a proposal: comments were due July 13, 2026, the Federal Register record showed no final rule, and section 157 of Public Law 119-103 provides that a rule arising from it shall not be issued, finalised or take effect through December 11, 2026 [1458] [1858] [1480]. Separately, FY 2027 budget requests, which are proposals, ask $0 for the Community Development Fund, which pays for CDBG, and $0 for each SAMHSA block grant, with both to be consolidated into a new block grant [1341] [535].
