## What it is

A newcomer tends to assume that the researcher with the idea is the applicant. On most federal awards an organisation applies and the investigator is named in its application; Grants.gov says that most grants are for organisations and that individuals can apply for opportunities from certain federal agencies [1896] [1895]. NSF invites proposals from all qualified scientists, engineers and educators through an eligible organisation, and unaffiliated individuals are not eligible for direct funding [158] [156]. At NIH any individual with the skills, knowledge and resources to carry out the research may be the programme director/principal investigator, shortened to PD/PI, while the organisation holds the registrations [4] [117].

The organisation also signs: on Grants.gov someone must hold the Authorized Organization Representative (AOR) role to submit, and the roles are assigned by the contact named in the organisation's SAM.gov registration [1895]. At NIH a Signing Official, a business official with authority to bind the organisation, registers it in eRA Commons and creates the accounts of its PD/PIs [1911] [1909] [1910]. NSF's Authorized Organizational Representative signs a proposal sent through Grants.gov and makes nine certifications under NSF's policy guide [161] [257]. NIH's help and planning pages send an applicant first to the organisation's own office of sponsored research, here called the sponsored programmes office, which explains the process and roles and sets up system accounts [146] [119].

Money meant for one person's training does not settle who applies, because each programme fixes who submits [818] [784] [1016] [825]. In NSF's Graduate Research Fellowship the student applies directly, though the funds are awarded to the institution where the fellow enrols [818]. NSF's biology postdoctoral fellowship is submitted by the individual and awarded directly to the fellow [837]. For an NIH F31 or F32 fellowship the organisation applies with the person named, and an institution likewise submits a mentored career development (K) application for a named candidate [784] [737] [744]. In the T32 training grant the person leaves the application altogether: an institution applies through a programme director and appoints trainees, who receive NIH stipends and do not apply to the funder [750] [732]. NIH sets the stipends for these National Research Service Awards (NRSA) once a fiscal year, and for awards made with FY 2026 funds the predoctoral level is $29,364 [732].

Some foundation awards put a nomination before any application. The Packard Foundation invited 50 institutions to nominate for its 2026 fellowships, up to two each through the president, and a faculty member elsewhere has no route in [2149]. Because the Dreyfus Foundation takes one nomination per institution a year and Pew one nominee from each participating institution, the institution runs an internal selection and the nominee then completes the funder's own application [2146] [2174] [2149]. An organisation can also take part in a federal award without applying for it: a subrecipient receives a subaward from a pass-through entity, the body that passes federal money on, to carry out part of the award, and the federal terms flow down to it [1799] [1805]. Where agencies stand on a fiscally sponsored applicant was read on two pages only, and neither accepts one [2065] [648]. The Arts Endowment bars an organisation without its own non-profit status from applying through a fiscal sponsor, and an NEH notice of March 12, 2026 makes fiscal sponsors applying on behalf of an ineligible organisation ineligible; other agencies' rules were not checked [2065] [648] [2047].

## The key idea in one picture

Picture the funder as a box on the right and, on the left, a person standing above an organisation. Four arrows cross from left to right, each labelled with who submits. The first leaves the organisation and reads *organisation applies, investigator named*: an NIH R01 or an NSF research proposal [4] [156]. The second also leaves the organisation and reads *organisation applies for one named person*: the NIH F31, the F32 and the mentored K awards [784] [737] [744]. The third leaves the person and reads *person applies*: NSF's Graduate Research Fellowship and its biology postdoctoral fellowship [818] [837]. The fourth leaves the organisation and bends back to a row of small figures beside it, labelled *organisation applies, then appoints its trainees*: the T32 [750].

Each arrow that leaves the organisation passes a seal marked *AOR or Signing Official*, the person who submits for it or binds it [1895] [1911]. Two marks sit off the arrows. A smaller box hangs beneath the organisation, labelled *subrecipient: a subaward from the applicant* [1799]. A gate stands in front of the person, drawn for some foundation awards only and labelled *nomination by an invited institution* [2149].

## How this guide uses it

The sections on how grant money moves and on money that follows a person lean on this idea, as do the routes for fellowships, career development awards, training grants, foundation scholar awards and joining another body's application. Each programme record says who may apply, and each registration record says whether a step falls to the organisation or to the person.
