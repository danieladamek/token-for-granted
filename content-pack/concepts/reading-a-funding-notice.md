## What it is

A newcomer might take a funding notice for an advertisement that each agency lays out as it likes. A notice of funding opportunity is the formal announcement that federal funding is available through a financial assistance programme, and the Uniform Guidance fixes what it must contain and in what order [1799] [1800] [1848]. Those rules cover discretionary programmes [1800]. The full text has nine sections: Basic Information; Eligibility; Program Description; Application Contents and Format; Submission Requirements and Deadlines; Application Review Information; Award Notices; Post-Award Requirements and Administration; and Other Information, which is optional [1848].

Ahead of the full text comes a summary, displayed on Grants.gov [1800]. It gives the agency name, the opportunity title, the announcement type, the funding opportunity number, the Assistance Listing number, funding details, key dates, an executive summary and agency contacts [1800]. The opportunity number identifies the notice: PA-25-301 is NIH's parent R01 notice [4]. The Assistance Listing number points to something else, the programme's entry in the public catalogue of federal assistance programmes kept by the General Services Administration at SAM.gov, and the same number is printed in the award document [1799] [1808] [1810].

Inside the full text, the Eligibility section has to list every type of eligible applicant [1848]. The dates show how long a notice stays open. The guidance sets a standard of at least 60 calendar days and a floor of 30 absent exigent circumstances, though the paragraph states an expectation, not a requirement [1800]. Cost sharing, also called matching, is the part of project cost not paid by federal funds; the notice must state whether it is required, and say so when it is not [1880] [1848]. The application package is the set of forms the opportunity requires, loaded into its Workspace on Grants.gov, where a team fills them in before an Authorized Organization Representative submits [1918] [1915]. Among the forms is the SF-424, the standard federal application form, issued in several sets [1918].

NIH and NSF each depart from the picture of one notice with one deadline. NIH's ordinary route is the parent announcement, a standing, broad notice for an application whose topic the investigator chose, and each is for one activity code [1] [145]. An activity code has three characters, the first usually marking the funding category: R for research, T for training, F for fellowship, K for career development [3]. The notice stays open for years while applications arrive on standard due dates, three a year for each code [2]. PA-25-301 was posted December 18, 2024; its NIH page gives an expiration date of January 08, 2028 and its Grants.gov listing a close date of January 7, 2028 [4] [112]. NSF publishes four kinds of opportunity [156]. A programme description and a programme announcement use the generic rules of its policy guide; a programme solicitation is more focused, applies for a limited period and is used when NSF departs from those rules, for instance by limiting who may submit [156] [158]. A Dear Colleague Letter gives information or announces interest in a topic [156] [158]. Each NSF opportunity sets its own timing: a deadline date, a target date, a submission window or acceptance at any time [156].

## The key idea in one picture

Picture one line running left to right, the life of a single listing on Grants.gov, with four marks on it. The marks carry the four values of the site's status filter: forecasted, posted, closed, archived [2536]. At Forecasted the listing gives estimated dates: the forecast for a NIOSH R21 notice, RFA-OH-27-033, gave a forecast posted date of July 27, 2026, an estimated post date of December 16, 2026 and an estimated application due date of February 16, 2027 [2535]. At Posted the listing gives a posted date and a close date, as the parent R01 listing does [112]. Two NIOSH notices posted May 8, 2026 with a close date of June 23, 2026 were shown as Closed [2536]. At Archived stands a NIOSH announcement that expired on November 21, 2025, its listing archived as of December 23, 2025 [556] [557].

Grants.gov's own definitions of the four words were not among the pages read for this guide; each mark rests on a listing that carried the word [2535] [2536] [557].

## How this guide uses it

The sections on how grant money moves and on the research funders both describe notices, and every route to a competed federal programme ends at one. Programme records give a notice's number, dates and cost-sharing line where a notice exists.
<!-- framing -->

Each programme record also carries a status label, which is the guide's own and describes a programme, not one listing. Open: a notice is in force and takes applications, or its next due date is still ahead. Forecast: no open notice, with a forecast or an announced date for the next. Closed: the current notice's last deadline has passed and the programme continues. Expired: the announcement lapsed, was archived or was ended early, and nothing replaces it. Not-competed: the funder says the competition is cancelled, paused or will not be held. No-current-notice: none was found for the current cycle, and the funder has not said the programme ended. Formula: money allotted by formula or block grant, with no federal competition a non-profit can enter. Unconfirmed: the pages would not load or did not settle it.

OMB's proposed rule of May 29, 2026 would require every federal funding opportunity to be posted on Grants.gov, with an exception where public announcement poses a national security risk, and would revise Appendix I [1803] [1458]. A rule arising from that proposal may not be issued or finalised through December 11, 2026 [1480].
