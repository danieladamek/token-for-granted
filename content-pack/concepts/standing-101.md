## What it is

A newcomer assumes that eligibility is a question about the project and the people who would carry it out. Many programmes ask an earlier question, about the organisation that applies, and this guide calls the answer standing. NIH's Institutional Development Award (IDeA) settles it by location, since an institution is eligible only if it is in a state NIH lists [1546]. The Education Department's programme for historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) is open to a class fixed by statute and by a list in regulation, whatever an institution's current enrolment [1649].

Location is the first kind. NSF's EPSCoR aims to raise the research competitiveness of targeted jurisdictions, each a state, territory or commonwealth, and Congress authorised IDeA in 1993 for states with historically low NIH grant funding success rates [866] [1545]. Several agencies keep such lists, and the lists are not the same [1517] [1565] [1576] [1543]. NSF names 28 jurisdictions; the Energy Department follows NSF's criteria, and for NASA jurisdiction eligibility is determined by NSF [1517] [1567] [1574]. Defense prints 36 entries in the year's notice, USDA lists 19 states for FY 2026, and NIH lists 23 states and Puerto Rico [1565] [1576] [1543]. A jurisdiction can be on one list and off another [1517] [1565] [1543] [1576].

A second kind is a designation, and a third is an institution's profile. HBCUs are listed by state in 34 CFR 608.2, and a tribal college or university is defined by statute [1649] [1617]. A Hispanic-serving institution (HSI) is an eligible institution whose undergraduate full-time-equivalent enrolment is at least 25 percent Hispanic at the end of the award year before it applies, and the status is shown at application, with no permanent list [1647] [1642]. Under Titles III and V of the Higher Education Act the Education Department designates each "eligible institution" yearly, from data on needy students and core expenses per student [1596] [1595]. NASA checks eligibility for its minority university project against its NASA MSI List [1629]. The NIH R15 AREA award is for an institution whose undergraduate enrolment is greater than its graduate enrolment and which has not received NIH support totalling more than $6 million per year in 4 of the last 7 years [7]. NSF counts as predominantly undergraduate an institution that awarded 20 or fewer Ph.D./D.Sci. degrees in NSF-supported fields over the previous two academic years combined, and it opens some programmes only to institutions that the Carnegie Classification does not place in R1 [254] [832].

For a non-profit the first standing is tax status. Grants.gov lists two non-profit applicant categories, those with 501(c)(3) status with the IRS and those without, each other than institutions of higher education [2064]. Recognition follows an application to the IRS on Form 1023 or Form 1023-EZ and is decided by determination letter [2046] [2059]. Every section 501(c)(3) organisation is classified as a private foundation or a public charity, and is presumed a private foundation unless it requests and qualifies for a determination as a public charity [2054]. Three consecutive years without an annual return or notice end the status by automatic revocation [2057].

## The key idea in one picture

A path runs from a box on the left, labelled *applicant organisation*, to a box on the right, labelled *review of the project*. A gate labelled *standing* crosses the path, and three keys hang on it. The first key is labelled *a list an agency publishes*: the NSF and NIH jurisdiction lists, and the Defense list in each year's notice [1517] [1543] [1565]. The second is labelled *a designation another agency grants*: an IRS determination letter, the Education Department's yearly eligibility designation, or the Bureau of Indian Education's determination for a new tribal college [2059] [1596] [1617]. The third is labelled *the institution's own certification*: the R15 letter signed by a provost or similar official, or the Certification of RUI/ROA Eligibility at NSF [45] [254].

A note under the first key says that the rule behind a list is not always printed. USDA states its test, which is funding no higher than the 38th percentile of all states on a three-year rolling average [1576]. The NIH pages and notices read do not state the rule by which a state enters or leaves the IDeA list, the Defense listings do not state theirs, and NSF's page says eligibility is frozen through FY 2027 [1543] [1545] [1565] [1517].

## How this guide uses it

The section on standing and capacity, the routes for EPSCoR and IDeA jurisdictions, for designated institutions and for becoming an eligible non-profit, and the standing records rest on this idea. Each standing record says who qualifies, how the standing is decided and which programmes it opens.
